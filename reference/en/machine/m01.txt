A year ago I became a team lead. Nobody handed me a manual, and a fair amount of what I thought I knew about leading turned out to be wrong.

Here is what the first year taught me:

1. Your job changes faster than your skills. The strongest engineer on the team is not automatically the strongest lead. Your output now comes through other people, which is a different job.

2. Clarity beats speed. When I rushed to hand out work, my direction was often vague. Taking ten minutes to write down what "done" means saved hours later.

3. Ask before you assume. I spent too long guessing why someone was quiet or slow. Usually a direct, early, kind question had the answer.

4. One-on-ones are not status meetings. The useful ones covered what the person wanted next, what was getting in their way, and what I could stop doing.

5. Protect the team from noise, not from information. Shielding people from everything made them feel out of the loop. Sharing context, including the bad news, built more trust.

6. Give feedback close to the moment. Waiting for the "right time" usually meant it never happened.

7. Your calendar is your strategy. If you don't block time for thinking and for your people, urgent work will fill every hour.

I still get things wrong every week. But the team's work is better than it was a year ago, and I am learning to measure my success by how well others can do their work without me.

If you have led people for a while, what is the one lesson you wish someone had told you earlier?
