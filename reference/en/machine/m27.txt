Onboarding a new team member is one of the most underrated things a team lead does. Most of the time, a new hire gets a laptop, a login, and a calendar invite for "welcome," and then is left to figure out the rest on their own. That costs weeks of productivity and, worse, quietly signals that they are on their own.

Here is the approach I've found works best.

Before day one, prepare the basics. Make sure accounts, tools, and access are ready before they arrive. Nothing kills the first morning faster than waiting two hours for a password reset. Write a short welcome note that covers what the first week looks like, what time to show up, and who to ask for what.

On day one, focus on people, not process. Introduce them to the team in a small, relaxed way. Pair them with a buddy: one person they can ask the "silly" questions without feeling judged. Skip the 40-slide company deck. Walk through how work actually gets done here, including the unwritten rules.

In the first two weeks, give them one small, real task. It should be useful to the team and finishable within a few days. Early wins build confidence faster than any amount of reading. Review the work together and be specific about what went well and what to adjust.

Set clear expectations for the first 30, 60, and 90 days. Write down what success looks like at each stage, and revisit it together. New hires should never have to guess whether they are doing well.

Schedule regular one-on-ones from the start, and keep them honest. Ask what is confusing, what takes longer than it should, and what they noticed that the team has stopped seeing. Fresh eyes are one of the most valuable things a new person brings, so make it easy for them to speak up.

Finally, document as you go. Every question a new hire asks is a sign that something is missing from your docs. Fix the gap once, and you save the next person the same confusion.

Good onboarding is not about making a new hire feel welcome for one day. It is about making them effective as fast as possible, and making sure they stay.
