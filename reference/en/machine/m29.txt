The worst boss I ever had taught me more about leading than any of the good ones.

He never raised his voice. He didn't need to. He just went quiet.

You'd send him a draft, and nothing came back for three days. Then a one-line reply: "Not what I expected." No word on what he did expect. You'd rewrite it, guessing. Another silence.

For about a year I thought the problem was me. I worked later. I over-prepared. I'd show up to meetings with three versions of the same slide in case he didn't like the first two.

Then a new person joined the team and went through exactly the same thing in her first month. Watching it happen to someone else, I finally saw it for what it was. He wasn't holding us to a high standard. He just never told anyone what the standard was.

Here's what I took from it, and still use:

1. Say what "good" looks like before the work starts. If you can't describe it, you're not ready to judge it.

2. Silence is feedback too, just the worst kind. People fill the gap with their own fears.

3. "Not what I expected" is about you, not them. Get specific or say nothing.

4. Your team's late nights aren't proof of commitment. Sometimes they're proof you're unclear.

I manage people now. Some weeks I catch myself typing a vague reply because I'm tired and it's quicker. That's when I think of him, delete it, and write the longer version.

He never knew he was teaching me anything. I'm grateful anyway.
