At 35, I walked away from a career I had spent 11 years building.

On paper, it made no sense. I had a title people respected, a salary I'd worked hard for, and a team that trusted me. What I didn't have was curiosity. I could do my job in my sleep, and some weeks I did.

So I started learning something new. Evenings. Weekends. Badly at first.

Here is what I wish someone had told me before I made the switch:

1. You are not starting from zero. Eleven years of managing people, handling deadlines and dealing with difficult clients did not disappear. They became my edge.

2. Being the beginner again is humbling, and that's a good thing. I asked more questions in my first month than in the previous five years.

3. The money dips. Plan for it. I saved for a year before I made the move, and I'm glad I did.

4. People will say "isn't it a bit late?" The answer is that 35 is not late. It's the middle of a 40-year career.

5. Nobody is watching as closely as you think. Most people are too busy worrying about their own path.

I'm not going to pretend it's been easy. There were months I doubted everything. But I'd take doubt about a path I chose over comfort in one I'd outgrown.

If you're sitting in a job that looks fine but feels flat, you're not being ungrateful. You're paying attention.

What's one thing you'd change careers to do, if you knew it would work?
