Last Friday I watched a teammate stay until 9pm to fix a bug that wasn't hers.

On Monday, nobody mentioned it.

That bothered me more than the bug did.

So I sent her a message. Not "great job!" Something specific: "Because you caught that edge case, the client demo ran without a single hiccup. I noticed, and it mattered."

Her reply came in four minutes: "I wasn't sure anyone saw that."

That's the thing about thank-yous at work. Most people are doing good work in a quiet room, wondering if anyone is looking.

A few things I've learned about saying thank you well:

1. Be specific. "Thanks for your help" fades. "Thanks for rewriting that onboarding doc so I could finally understand the process" sticks.

2. Name the impact. People want to know their work changed something, not just that it was noticed.

3. Say it fast. Gratitude has a short shelf life. A thank-you two days later beats one in the next quarterly review.

4. Say it up, not just down. Managers need to hear it too. They rarely do.

5. Say it in public when you can. Recognition in front of others travels further than a private note.

It costs nothing. It takes under a minute. And it changes how people show up the next day.

Who helped you recently and never heard about it?

Go tell them today.
