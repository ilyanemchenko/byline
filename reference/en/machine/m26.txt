The first job offer I ever got, I said yes before the recruiter finished the sentence.

I was so relieved someone wanted me that I didn't ask a single question. A year later I found out a colleague hired the same month, for the same role, was making 14% more. She had simply asked.

That gap didn't close for three years. Every raise was a percentage of a number I never pushed on.

Here's what I do differently now, and what I tell anyone who asks me:

1. Never give the first number if you can avoid it.
"What are you expecting?" can be answered with "I'd like to understand the full scope of the role first. What range did you have budgeted?" Most recruiters will tell you.

2. Know your range before the call, not during it.
Check levels.fyi, Glassdoor, industry surveys, and ask two or three people in similar roles. Walk in with data, not a feeling.

3. Pause after they make the offer.
Say "Thank you, I'm excited about this. Can I have a day to look at the full package?" Silence and time are not rude. They're normal.

4. Ask for one specific thing, with a reason.
"Based on the scope we discussed and the market data I've seen, I was hoping for something closer to X." One clear ask beats a list of complaints.

5. Look beyond base salary.
Signing bonus, equity, title, start date, remote days, learning budget, a review at six months instead of twelve. If base is fixed, something else usually isn't.

6. Remember they already chose you.
By the time there's an offer, the company has spent weeks and real money on you. A polite counter almost never makes an offer disappear.

The worst outcome of asking is usually "this is our best offer." The worst outcome of not asking compounds every year.
