For years I counted being busy as being productive.

I had three work tabs open, a chat window blinking in the corner, and a half-written document I kept switching away from. At the end of the day I felt exhausted, yet I could not point to one thing I had actually finished.

So I stopped multitasking. Not all at once, and not perfectly, but on purpose.

Here is what changed:

1. One task per block. I pick one piece of work and keep it on screen until it is done or I have written down the next step.
2. Notifications off by default. Messages get checked at set times, not every time a dot appears.
3. Fewer open loops. Before starting something new, I finish or consciously park what I already have.

What I noticed in the first few weeks:

- Tasks took less total time, because I stopped paying the cost of switching back and forth.
- My mistakes dropped, especially in writing and code, where most of them came from a distracted reread of a sentence or a line.
- The day felt calmer, with fewer decisions made in the moment.

Multitasking feels efficient because each switch gives a small hit of progress. But attention has a cost every time you move it, and most of us pay that cost dozens of times a day without noticing.

If you want to try this, start small. Tomorrow morning, choose the single most important task, close everything unrelated, and work on it for 50 minutes. Notice how it feels.
