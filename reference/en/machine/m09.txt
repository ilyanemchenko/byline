The best career advice I ever got took four minutes and cost my mentor nothing.

I was two years into my first real job and convinced I needed to switch teams. I had a long list of reasons. My mentor listened, then asked one question: "Are you leaving something, or going toward something?"

I didn't have an answer. So I stayed, figured out what I actually wanted, and moved a year later for the right reasons instead of the loud ones.

That's what good mentorship looks like to me. It's rarely a grand plan or a polished framework. It's someone who has seen a few more chapters of the story asking the question you're too close to ask yourself.

A few things I've learned from both sides of the table:

1. Mentors don't hand you answers. They make your thinking sharper. The best ones ask more than they tell.

2. You don't need one perfect mentor. A peer who's six months ahead, a former manager, someone in a different industry: each sees a different blind spot.

3. Show up with something specific. "Can you mentor me?" is hard to say yes to. "Can I get 20 minutes on how you handled your first promotion conversation?" is easy.

4. Mentoring others teaches you as much as being mentored. Explaining what you know forces you to find out whether you actually know it.

5. Close the loop. Tell people what you did with their advice. It's the simplest thank-you, and it's why they'll pick up the phone next time.

None of us got where we are alone. Someone opened a door, made an intro, or asked the right question at the right moment.

Who was that person for you? And who could you be that person for this year?
