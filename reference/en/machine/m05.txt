Remote work doesn't make you more productive. Clear work does.

After years of working with distributed teams, I've noticed the same pattern. The teams that thrive remotely aren't the ones with the best tools. They're the ones who got specific about three things.

1. Outcomes over hours.
If your manager can't say what "done" looks like this week, no amount of green status dots will help. Write the outcome down. Share it. Check it on Friday.

2. Async by default, meetings by exception.
A 30-minute call for a status update costs six people three hours of context switching in total. A short written update costs one person ten minutes. Save live time for decisions, disagreements, and relationships.

3. Protected focus time.
Remote work removes the commute and adds a hundred small pings. The fix isn't willpower. It's agreeing as a team on when it's fine to be unreachable.

One more thing: remote work shifts the burden of clarity onto the writer. If your messages need three follow-ups, the problem usually isn't the reader.

Remote work isn't a perk or a problem. It's a spotlight. It shows you exactly where your team's communication was already weak.
