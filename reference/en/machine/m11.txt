I spent a year posting daily and got almost nothing back.

Then I changed one thing. I stopped writing about what I do and started writing about what I got wrong.

One post about a client project that went sideways got more replies than the previous 80 combined. People didn't want my highlight reel. They wanted to know whether someone like them could survive the same mistake.

Here is what I'd tell anyone starting a personal brand:

1. Pick one reader, not an audience. "Marketing leaders" is a crowd. "The head of marketing at a 40-person company who has no budget for an agency" is a person. Write to her.

2. Say the thing you'd say to a friend over coffee. Polished advice sounds like everyone else's. Specific stories sound like you.

3. Be consistent before you are good. The first 30 posts are practice. Nobody remembers them, and you need them.

4. Reply to people before you ask them to read you. Ten thoughtful comments a day on other people's posts built more of my network than any post I wrote.

5. Don't confuse reach with trust. A thousand strangers who scroll past matter less than ten people who would recommend you by name.

A personal brand isn't a logo or a tagline. It's what people say about you when you're not in the room.
