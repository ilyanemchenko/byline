I once froze mid-sentence in front of forty people and forgot my own name.

Not my slide. Not my point. My name.

For years I told myself the fear was about being bad at speaking. It wasn't. I was fine in rehearsal. I was fine with one person across a table. The fear showed up only when a room turned to look at me.

What I eventually understood: I wasn't afraid of speaking. I was afraid of being seen getting it wrong, with no chance to edit.

That reframe helped more than any tip I'd read. A few things that actually moved the needle for me:

1. Start with the first 30 seconds, memorized cold. Once the opening is automatic, the shaking fades before the real content begins.

2. Talk to one person at a time. Pick a friendly face, finish a thought, then move to another. A crowd is scary. A conversation isn't.

3. Pauses feel like disasters to you and like thinking to everyone else. Silence reads as confidence far more often than you'd expect.

4. Say yes to small stages. A team update. A toast. A 5-minute demo. Fear shrinks through repetition, not through a pep talk.

5. Stop trying to be fearless. Nerves and excitement feel nearly identical in the body. I now tell myself "I'm excited" and, oddly, it works.

I still get a knot in my stomach before I walk on. I've just stopped treating it as a warning sign. It's a sign that the thing matters to me.

If you avoid speaking because of this fear, you're in very large company. What helped you, or what still gets in your way?
