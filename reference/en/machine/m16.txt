Most people don't leave a job. They leave a manager.

After years of working with managers, leading teams, and being led by people I learned from, I keep coming back to the same few traits that separate a good manager from a great one.

1. They make the goal clear.
Great managers can explain what success looks like in one or two sentences. Their teams never have to guess what matters most this quarter.

2. They protect focus.
They decide what not to do. They absorb the noise, push back on meetings that don't need to exist, and give their people long stretches of uninterrupted time to do real work.

3. They give feedback early and specifically.
Waiting for the annual review to mention a problem is a failure of management. Great managers say "here is what happened, here is the impact, here is what I need next time" the same week it happens.

4. They share credit and take responsibility.
When things go well, the team gets the spotlight. When things go wrong, the manager looks at their own decisions first.

5. They care about growth more than comfort.
They ask what their people want to become, then give them stretch work with enough support to succeed. Some of their best hires will eventually leave for bigger roles, and they treat that as a win.

6. They are honest, even when it is uncomfortable.
People can handle hard news. What they can't handle is finding out later that they were kept in the dark.

Management is not a promotion for being the best individual contributor. It is a different job, with a different measure of success: how well your people do their best work without you.

What is the best manager you have ever had, and what did they do that others didn't?
