Before I started my first business, I thought the hard part would be the idea. It wasn't. Here is what I wish I had known.

1. Talking to customers is not optional research. My early assumptions were wrong in small but expensive ways. Ask people what they did last week to deal with the problem, not whether they would buy your solution.

2. Revenue fixes more problems than features do. I spent months polishing things nobody had asked for. Getting one person to pay taught me more than any roadmap did.

3. Cash runway is a decision tool, not a spreadsheet exercise. Know exactly how many months you have, and decide in advance what you will stop doing when the number gets close.

4. Saying no is a core skill. Every yes to a side request was a no to the thing that actually mattered.

5. Your energy is a business asset. Burnout arrived before the business did, and it cost me weeks I can't get back. Protect your sleep and build routines that don't depend on motivation.

6. Ask for help earlier. People who have already been through it are usually glad to talk, and one honest 30-minute conversation saved me from mistakes I would have paid for.

The idea was rarely the bottleneck. Distribution, decisions, and discipline were.

If you are starting something now, what is the one thing you wish someone had told you?
