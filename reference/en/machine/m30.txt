Founders don't burn out in one dramatic week. It happens in small trades that feel reasonable at the time.

You skip the walk because the roadmap is on fire. You answer Slack at 11 p.m. "just this once." You cancel dinner with your partner, then your friends, then the one thing that used to keep you sane.

For a long time I believed that was the price of building something. Some of it is. But I've started to think that balance isn't a reward you earn after the company works. It's part of how the company works.

A few things that have helped me:

1. Decide your non-negotiables before your calendar fills up. Mine are one full day off a week and dinner at home on most weeknights. Everything else gets negotiated.

2. Treat your energy as the real constraint, not your hours. A tired founder makes slow, expensive decisions. A rested one ships faster and fixes mistakes sooner.

3. Make "off" visible to your team. If you never log off, your people will never feel allowed to. Say when you're out, and mean it.

4. Keep one relationship and one habit that have nothing to do with work. When the business gets hard, that's what keeps you grounded and reminds you why you started.

Nobody is going to hand you permission to slow down. You have to set the boundaries yourself, then defend them when a crisis shows up on a Sunday.
