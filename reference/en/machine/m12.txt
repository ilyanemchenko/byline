At kilometre 32 of my first marathon, I stopped caring about my finish time.

I'd trained for 18 weeks. I had a pace plan taped to my wrist. And around the 20-mile mark, my legs simply filed a complaint the plan hadn't accounted for.

I finished. Slower than I wanted. But the race taught me more about building a business than most books I've read.

Three things I keep coming back to:

1. The race is won in the boring weeks.
Nobody sees the Tuesday 8 km in the rain. Nobody claps for the long run you didn't skip. Business is the same. The launch gets the attention, but the result was decided by the dull, repeated work months before it.

2. Going out too fast is the most common mistake.
Almost every first-timer does it. Adrenaline, crowds, fresh legs. You bank time early and pay it back with interest later. I've seen companies do the same: hire ahead of demand, spend ahead of revenue, promise ahead of capacity. Pace for the distance you're actually running.

3. Your plan will break. Have a rule for when it does.
My wrist plan was useless after kilometre 30. What saved me was a simpler rule I'd set in advance: if it falls apart, walk the water stations and run everything else. In business, the plan rarely survives the first quarter. A clear fallback rule is worth more than a detailed forecast.

One more thing. At the finish, nobody asked my time. They asked if I'd do it again.

The answer was yes. Same with the business.
