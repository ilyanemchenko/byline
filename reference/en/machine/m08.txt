I thought burnout meant working too much. I was wrong.

For me it looked like working normally while feeling nothing. Same meetings, same output, same smile on calls. But the work I used to love felt like carrying a wet coat uphill.

Here is what finally helped:

1. I named it early. Not "I'm tired" but "I've been dreading Mondays for three months." Specific words made it real enough to act on.

2. I cut one thing, not ten. Burnout advice often reads like a life overhaul. I dropped a single recurring commitment and got four hours back every week.

3. I stopped treating rest as a reward. Rest is maintenance. You don't wait for the engine to fail before changing the oil.

4. I told one person the truth. My manager's response was far better than the story I had made up in my head.

5. I protected one boundary. No email after 7pm. Boring, small, and it worked.

Recovery wasn't a dramatic retreat. It was months of small, unglamorous corrections.

If you're running on fumes right now: you don't need to prove you can handle more. You're allowed to change the load.
