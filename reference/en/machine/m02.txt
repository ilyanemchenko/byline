Most meetings should be emails.

Last quarter I counted. Of 22 recurring meetings on my calendar, 15 followed the same pattern: one person talks, everyone else listens, and the only "decision" is "sounds good."

That's not a meeting. That's a broadcast with a calendar invite.

Here's why email (or a short doc) wins for most of them:

1. It respects time zones and focus. A written update gets read when people are ready, not when the calendar says so. No one has to leave deep work for a status check.

2. Writing forces clarity. If you can't explain the update, the ask, or the decision in five sentences, a meeting won't save you. It will just hide the fuzziness for an hour.

3. You get a record for free. No "who agreed to what?" three weeks later. The thread is the memory.

4. Quiet people get a real voice. In a meeting, the loudest idea often wins. In writing, the best-argued one has a chance.

5. The math is brutal. A 30-minute meeting with 8 people costs 4 hours of work time. A 5-minute email costs 5 minutes to write and about 2 to read.

Keep the meeting for what it's actually good at:
- Real disagreement that needs live back-and-forth
- Sensitive conversations where tone matters
- Brainstorming where ideas build on each other
- Building trust in a new team

My rule now: if the agenda is "updates," it's an email. If there's a decision to argue about, send the context first, then meet for 20 minutes instead of 60.

Since we started doing this, we dropped from 22 recurring meetings to 9. Nobody missed the other 13.

What's one meeting on your calendar this week that could have been an email?
