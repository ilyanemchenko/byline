My first sales call went badly, and it taught me more than the next ten went well.

I prepared a demo. Twelve slides, a clean walkthrough, answers ready for every objection I could imagine. Then I spent about 25 of the 30 minutes talking.

The person on the other end was polite. They nodded. They said "interesting" twice. They never booked a second call.

Here is what I took from it.

1. The call is not about your product.
I thought I was there to show what I had built. They were there to see whether I understood their problem. I never asked what it was.

2. Silence is not a sign you're losing.
Every time there was a pause, I filled it. Those pauses were where they would have told me something real.

3. Questions beat slides.
The best moment of the call was when they said, "Well, what we actually struggle with is..." and I talked right over it to get to slide seven. I still think about that.

4. "Interesting" means no.
Polite interest is not interest. If nobody asks about price, timing or next steps, nothing is moving. I needed to ask for the next step instead of hoping for it.

5. Write down their words, not yours.
Afterwards I had pages of notes about what I said and almost nothing about what they said. The useful part of a call is their language, because that is what your next message should sound like.

On the next call I put the slides away, asked three questions and kept quiet. It was shorter, and it was the first one that ended with "can you send me more details?"

If you remember your first sales call, what did it teach you?
